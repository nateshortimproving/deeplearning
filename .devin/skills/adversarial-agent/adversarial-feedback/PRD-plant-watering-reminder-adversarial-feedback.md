# PRD Output file being judged: /Users/nateshort/Desktop/ImprovingDeepLearning/PRD-plant-watering-reminder.md

## Missing Requirements

- **Due-date anchoring rule.** The single most consequential product decision is unspecified: when a plant is marked watered, does the next due date reset from *today* or from the *original due date*? A plant due Monday watered Friday could next be due Friday+N or Monday+N — these diverge forever and produce meaningfully different plant health outcomes. Neither the requirements nor the open questions address it.
- **No re-notification / nag policy for overdue plants.** The spec fires one notification "on the day a plant is due" and then relies on visual highlighting — which the user only sees if they open the app unprompted, defeating the point of reminders. For the named "busy professional" audience who may miss a single notification, there is no requirement for repeat reminders on overdue plants.
- **Notification lifecycle management.** No requirements cover cancelling or rescheduling pending notifications when a plant is marked watered early, edited (frequency change), snoozed, or deleted. Without this, stale notifications fire for already-watered or deleted plants — a guaranteed v1 defect.
- **Undo / correction for accidental "watered" taps.** The flagship interaction is a one-tap notification action, which is maximally prone to accidental taps (lock screen, pocket). There is no undo, no way to correct a mis-logged watering, and no way to edit history entries. A single accidental tap corrupts the schedule with no recovery path.
- **Timezone and travel handling.** "Busy professionals who travel" are a named target audience, yet nothing specifies whether due dates and notification times anchor to the home timezone, follow the device timezone, or use absolute times. Crossing a date line can make a plant appear due a day early or late.
- **Android battery-optimization / OEM killer mitigation.** On Xiaomi/Huawei/Oppo and stock Android Doze, scheduled local notifications and alarms are routinely killed or deferred. This is the most common real-world failure mode for exactly this kind of app and gets no requirement (exact-alarm permission request, battery-optimization exemption prompt, or WorkManager fallback strategy).
- **Custom/user-defined species.** The assumptions mention a "free-text fallback" for species, but no functional requirement defines it. Can a user save a custom species with its own default frequency? Does free text get the suggested-default treatment? Unspecified.
- **Input validation bounds.** No requirement constrains the watering frequency field — 0, negative, fractional, or 99,999 days are all unaddressed. Same for plant name (empty, 10,000-char, duplicate names).
- **Onboarding / permission-flow requirements.** The assumptions say the app "should gate reminders on permission" and a success metric depends on an "onboarding survey" baseline — but no functional requirement describes an onboarding flow, a permission priming screen, or the survey itself. Metrics assume features nobody was asked to build.
- **Analytics/telemetry instrumentation.** Four of six success metrics (30-day retention, 60% confirmation-within-24h, <5% notification disable rate, overdue-days reduction) are unmeasurable without event logging — yet the app is specified as fully offline with no backend. Either a local-analytics-plus-consent requirement or an explicit decision to forgo measurement is missing entirely.
- **Restore/reinstall behavior.** If the OS restores app data from an iCloud/Google device backup (or the user reinstalls), plant data may return while scheduled notifications do not. No requirement covers rescheduling notifications on first launch after restore or data-schema versioning.
- **Accessibility.** No mention of VoiceOver/TalkBack labeling for the plant list and notification actions, Dynamic Type/text scaling, or sufficient contrast for the "overdue" visual highlight — which, if conveyed by color alone, fails colorblind users.
- **Notification content spec.** What the notification says (plant name, species, how overdue) is never specified, nor is behavior when multiple plants are due — which is exactly the unresolved digest-vs-per-plant question leaking into un-specified territory.

## Logical Errors

- **100-plant support vs. the 64-notification cap.** The success metric requires supporting ≥100 plants; the constraints acknowledge iOS caps pending local notifications at 64. The PRD says "schedule rolling reminders accordingly" but never resolves the consequence: if the user doesn't open the app, reminders beyond the scheduled window *never fire* — silently breaking the core "system shall send a notification when a plant is due" requirement for any plant outside the window. The guarantee and the constraint cannot both hold without a defined rescheduling strategy (e.g., repeating notifications, or scheduling on notification delivery).
- **Requirements depend on an unresolved open question.** The snooze requirement ("remind me tomorrow") and the watered-from-notification requirement both presuppose per-plant notifications with action buttons — while Open Question 1 still debates a single daily digest, under which per-plant notification actions are impossible. The open question should have been resolved *before* freezing requirements that depend on its answer.
- **"Push notification" terminology error.** The PRD repeatedly says "push notifications" but specifies local notifications in constraints. These are different APIs with different capabilities (push requires a server the PRD explicitly excludes). The wrong term invites a wrong implementation.
- **Metrics contradict the offline/no-backend architecture.** "≥70% of users who add a plant still have the app installed after 30 days" and "≥60% of watering reminders confirmed within 24 hours" require cross-user telemetry that a fully-offline, no-backend app cannot produce. The metrics and the architecture are mutually exclusive as written.
- **User story vs. metric mismatch on setup time.** The user story demands adding a plant "in under 30 seconds"; the success metric sets the bar at a *median* under 45 seconds. Either 30s is the target or 45s is — the metric is 50% looser than the story it supposedly validates.
- **"<5% disable notifications" denominator is undefined.** Does this count users who deny the OS permission prompt at onboarding (never enabling), users who toggle off in-app, or only users who enabled then disabled via system settings? Each interpretation yields a wildly different number, and the assumption says most users who deny upfront may never be counted.
- **Overdue metric presupposes a survey that doesn't exist.** "Decreases by ≥50% compared to a self-reported baseline in onboarding survey" requires an onboarding survey that appears nowhere in functional requirements or scope — and self-reported recall of "average days overdue" is methodologically near-meaningless as a baseline anyway.
- **"No accounts required to start" vs. "accounts out of scope."** The solution's "no accounts required *to start*" implies accounts may appear mid-flow in v1; Scope then defers all accounts to v2. The phrasings contradict on whether any account prompt can exist.
- **Watering history fields are ambiguous after snooze.** The log records "date watered, date due" — but if a plant was snoozed twice, which "date due" is logged: original due, first snooze, or last snooze? The auditability value of the history depends on the answer, and it's unspecified.
- **Suggested defaults undercut the problem statement.** The PRD's own problem statement says fixed generic schedules cause over/under-watering — then solves it with a *generic per-species* schedule that ignores pot size, light, soil, and season (explicitly out of scope). The suggested default can be as wrong as the calendar reminders it criticizes, with no requirement to communicate uncertainty or let users correct it over time.

## Edge Cases

- **Watering early.** A user waters 3 days before the due date and taps "watered." Does the schedule reset from today (correct) or stay anchored? Combined with the missing anchoring rule, early watering can compound schedule drift.
- **User away for weeks.** A traveling busy professional misses a due date by 10 days. Does the app re-notify daily, once, or never? If once, the plant dies silently; if daily, notification fatigue. The PRD specifies neither, and open question 2 only asks it half-heartedly.
- **Timezone crossing / DST.** Date math done as `lastWatered + N*24h` instead of calendar days drifts across DST transitions, moving the due day by an hour that can flip the calendar date — and "configurable time of day" behaves unexpectedly when the 9am slot shifts across zones.
- **Stale notification actions.** User marks a plant watered in-app, then taps "watered" again on the still-displayed lock-screen notification — does it double-log and push the schedule out twice? Or taps "watered" on a notification for a since-deleted plant — crash or silent no-op? Both need defined behavior.
- **Photo permission denied / storage failure.** Photos are in scope and depend on camera/library permission, but nothing covers denial (does the plant save photoless?), storage-full on save, or orphaned photo files after edit/delete.
- **Frequency edit mid-cycle.** User changes a plant from every-7-days to every-14-days while it's 5 days from due. Recompute from last watered (now overdue), from edit date, or prompt? Unspecified — and each choice produces surprising behavior for some user.
- **Device clock manipulation.** User changes the device date backward/forward (or a wrong auto-time sync occurs); every plant's due status and scheduled notifications are computed against a moving clock. No tolerance strategy specified.
- **Snooze indefinitely / snooze on deleted plant.** Can a user snooze the same plant 30 days running (effectively disabling it while looking "active")? Does a pending snoozed notification for a deleted plant still fire? Unbounded snoozing and dangling snooze notifications are unaddressed.
- **iOS notification cap exhaustion at the limit.** With >64 plants (or 64 plants plus snoozed/digest notifications competing for slots), which notifications get dropped — soonest due, and is the policy deterministic? "Schedule rolling reminders accordingly" is a hand-wave, not a policy.
- **Fresh install race: due today.** User adds a plant that was last watered longer ago than its frequency (a real plant already overdue). Nothing covers the "already overdue on day one" path — does it notify immediately, show highlighted, or wait a cycle?
- **Notification action while device locked/offline mid-flight.** Tapping "watered" requires the action to commit locally without the app opening; if the write fails (storage error), the schedule silently doesn't reset. No failure handling specified.
- **OS backup restore with phantom schedules.** Restored plant data without restored notification schedules (or vice versa — notifications firing for plants whose data wasn't restored) needs a reconcile-on-launch requirement.

## Quality Gaps

- **No acceptance criteria or testability.** Requirements like "overdue plants visually highlighted" and "function fully offline" have no pass/fail definition. Each functional requirement needs at least one verifiable criterion to be implementable and reviewable.
- **No prioritization.** Every requirement is weighted identically. The digest-vs-per-plant open question, history log, and snooze are clearly different stakes, but nothing marks what v1 can ship without.
- **Open questions carry load-bearing decisions.** Digest vs. per-plant changes the notification architecture, action design, and snooze semantics; unacknowledged-reschedule changes the overdue model; monetization isn't needed for the spec but tablet layout changes UI scope. These are resolved-before-build questions presented as optional.
- **Missing non-functional requirements.** Beyond one scroll-frame metric, nothing bounds app launch time, notification scheduling latency, local storage footprint (photos grow unbounded), or battery impact of the scheduling strategy.
- **Species dataset dependency is unowned.** The "curated species→frequency dataset" gates the new-plant-parent value proposition, but has no owner, no sourcing plan ("in-house or licensed" is not a plan), no update mechanism in a no-backend app, and no fallback if it slips.
- **Metrics lack a measurement plan.** No instrumentation requirement, no consent/privacy story for analytics in a privacy-positioned local-only app, and no method for "app still installed" detection without a backend. The metrics section is aspirational, not operational.
- **No failure-state spec.** Storage write failures (watered log, photo save), notification scheduling failures (cap hit, permission revoked mid-life), and corrupted local data all lack defined behavior.
- **Localization unaddressed.** Species names, notification copy, and date formats are inherently localizable; a predefined English species list silently assumes an English-only v1 that is never stated.
- **Schema migration ignored.** Local persistence plus a stated v2 cloud-sync direction means the v1 schema is a long-lived commitment; nothing requires versioning or forward-compatible design now, when it's cheap.
- **Undefined terms.** "Visually highlighted," "predefined list," "rolling reminders," and "degrade gracefully" are used as if specified. Each hides a design decision the reader must guess at.
