## Focus
Final week. Embedded Engineer completes work with the Testing team, follows up on commitment statements from Session 6, and completes the Program Completion Rubric for all participants. The program closes with artifacts that stay in the codebase and behaviors that outlast the Embedded Engineer's departure.

---

## Embedded Engineer Focus (Testing Team + All)
- Day 1: Commitment statement check-in. Review each participant's Week 11 commitment statement with them individually (15 min each). Have they started? What got in the way? What would it take to still do it this week?
- Days 2–3: Final artifact polish. Every committed artifact should meet the handoff standard: committed, commented, transferable, tested by someone other than the author.
- Day 4: Program Completion Rubric conversations. Embedded Engineer meets with each participant (30 min) to discuss their rubric assessment. Share the Embedded Engineer's evaluation. Hear the participant's self-assessment. Agree on the result.
- Day 5: Program close. Final retrospective with all participants. Embedded Engineer's closing remarks. Handoff document.

## Handoff Document
The Embedded Engineer produces a brief handoff document for the client team (and client stakeholder if applicable) covering:
- What was built: list of all committed artifacts, by team
- What changed: summary of behavior changes observed over 12 weeks
- What participants can do independently: specific capabilities, not vague claims
- What comes next: continued adoption of the core DLP capabilities (skills, adversarial agents) and, for teams ready to invest, a future Stage 4 Deep Learning Program covering custom harnesses and production workflow integration — what it would take and what resources exist
- Open questions or items that need continued attention

This document is a program deliverable, not an internal-only artifact.

## Program Completion Criteria (Reference)
See [Program-Completion-Rubric](../../Resources/Measures-and-Assessments/Program-Completion-Rubric.md) for full rubric. Summary:

| Criterion | What "Met" Looks Like |
|-----------|----------------------|
| A — Craft | At least one skill and adversarial agent committed and independently buildable |
| B — Evaluate | Python evaluation script with LLM judge, 3+ iterations documented |
| C — Iterate | Evidence-driven iteration as a practice, not a one-time event |
| D — Adopt | At least one workflow genuinely changed as default behavior |

**Completion requires all four criteria at "Met" or better.**

Program completion also correlates with the AI Adoption Maturity Assessment (7 dimensions, 21 questions): participants achieving DLP Completion will typically score at 3.0+ averages across dimensions 1–4 on their final assessment.

## Embedded Engineer Coaching Notes
- Week 12 is the hardest week to stay disciplined. There's a natural pull toward celebration and summarizing — which is fine, but not at the expense of the actual completion work.
- The handoff document matters. The client stakeholder is thinking "what did we get for 12 weeks?" The handoff document answers that question with specifics.
- The internal program summary is how Improving improves the Deep Learning Program (DLP) over time. Be honest about what didn't work. The next Embedded Engineer cohort benefits from real observations, not polished retrospectives.
- The last thing the Embedded Engineer should say to each participant: "Here's the one thing I want you to keep doing when I'm gone." Make it specific to that person. Make it the thing you've seen them struggle with and overcome. It's the most personalized coaching you'll do in the whole program.
- **Transfer model note:** The handoff document should clearly state which skills have been transferred from the workspace to the real codebase and which are still workspace-only. The workspace stays with the team after the engagement — it's a reference they can return to. But the real value is in the skills that made it to the real codebase. Document the transfer status of each skill: "transferred and in use," "transferred but needs iteration," or "workspace-only, not yet transferred." This gives the team a clear picture of what to focus on after the EE departs.
