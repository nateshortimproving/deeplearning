# Embedded Engineer Playbook

*Field-tested coaching plays from Wave 1 Embedded Engineers. These are practical techniques — use what fits your engagement.*

---

## Rough Process Mapping

**Use When:** Early in engagement, ideally before Workshop 2

**Target Outcome:** Rough outline of team's work process from story to working code. Targets for missing prompts to write.

**Steps in Brief:**
1. On a whiteboard, have the team start listing all the steps — everything they do starting with a Story and proceeding until they push code into a PR. Capture the steps on sticky notes for later. (You can use "Verb Object Context" format if unsure where to begin, e.g., *Create Acceptance Criteria*, *Add Criteria to Jira Ticket*, *Plan Implementation*, etc.)
2. Help the team sequence the actions and draw out what information passes between actions.
3. Any time someone mentions "AND" in an action description — either of the work or of the products produced — have them split at the AND into two different prompts.

## Divide and Conquer

**Use When:** A team has widely separated experience levels with AI (early adopters mixed with laggards)

**Target Outcome:** Feed the front-runners so they grow faster. Prevent the laggards from hiding behind the front-runners in group settings. Give the entire team the support they need to grow at the level they can engage with.

**Steps in Brief:**
1. Identify the maturity stages of all players in your group
2. Invite the front-runners to additional 1:1 coaching, and remove them from the next group session
3. Be sure to communicate to the front-runner that this is recognition of their growth to help them grow even faster
4. In the next, smaller group session, actively solicit work from, and collaborate with, the remaining players at their level or depth

## Goals → Measures → Actions Discussion

**Use When:** You want to turn the team's focus away from just optimizing their part, towards optimizing whole team process towards real business goals.

**Target Outcome:** Team defines their target goals, measures to indicate progress, and identifies actions they can take to impact their goals (AI or otherwise).

**Steps in Brief:**
1. Start with a question: *"Why are we {doing X} from the business's perspective? Why spend money doing what we do?"* Push for dollars and cents answers where possible. Have the group answer on sticky notes. After you have a handful of answers, start grouping them.
2. Now challenge the group: *"What measures could we use to demonstrate we're meeting each of these goals?"* Doesn't mean we can measure it today — just what could we track to indicate how we're doing. After your group has answered, ask them which measures are things we want to **improve** (Pursuit Metrics) and which do we need to **keep at a good level** (Sustain Metrics). Discuss the relationship between the metrics.
3. After identifying Pursuit and Sustain Metrics, identify at most 3 pairs (Pursuit, Sustain) of metrics your team would use to see progress on their goals. For the most important pair, have the group define what they should do in each of these 4 circumstances:
   - Pursuit improves, Sustain improves (or stable)
   - Pursuit improves, Sustain drops
   - Pursuit drops, Sustain improves (or stable)
   - Pursuit drops, Sustain drops

## An Exercise After Each Workshop

**Use When:** After each workshop, to provide targeted practice of each concept

**Target Outcome:** Teams get practice in each core concept in a controlled, repeatable (for the Embedded Engineer) way.

**Comments:**
- The exercises are really fun for show-and-tell. Everyone gets the same PRD and roughly has the same commands, but the finished products all look distinctly different — so we get to have the conversation about how if you don't make it a requirement, AI will make that part up.
- Over time, they've got to see that in action, and the end result of Exercise 3 usually looks pretty close to the other variants that others have made because we actually start focusing on UX guidance when making the stories.
- The exercises have also become the unofficial starting points for a lot of commands they actually use for their work. The original story creation command is still used today to great effect. They added their own spin here and there to support what the team needed.

## Build Your Own SDLC Pipeline

**Use When:** When not engaged in coaching

**Target Outcome:** Mastery of DLP content, identify the next hurdle your students will hit.

**Steps in Brief:**
1. Do the work from each workshop yourself
2. Build out your own SDLC workflows (from start to finish)
3. Pay attention to the challenges and struggles you encounter
4. Document how you resolve them
5. When team members hit similar challenges, share your SDLC pipeline and solution approach

## Everyone Gets a Turn at Show-and-Tell

**Use When:** Regularly throughout the DLP during group collaboration sessions, especially early on

**Target Outcome:** Identify team members who are struggling before they fall behind.

**Steps in Brief:**
- Push for show-and-tell with all members of every call (can be exercises or things they're actually doing for the client)

---

## One Meeting, One Artifact

**Use When:** Each 1:1 with team members

**Target Outcome:** Each 1:1 ends with a working artifact.

**Comments:**
- Making every 1:1 end with a working artifact. Instead of leaving sessions with "here's what to try," we finish with a refined command, a workflow breakdown, or an evaluation result in hand. Pod members leave with something they built, not a todo list, and it shows up in their outcome reports.

---

## Cross-Pollination

**Use When:** Throughout the DLP

**Target Outcome:** Good ideas from one team propagate across the group. All affected teams benefit from innovation in any team.

**Comments:**
- Pulling patterns from one pod into the next pod's session. When Team A does something that works (like someone's shared evaluations), bring it into Team B's or Team C's session as a "here's what another team just tried." Keeps cross-pollination happening without needing a formal channel for it.
