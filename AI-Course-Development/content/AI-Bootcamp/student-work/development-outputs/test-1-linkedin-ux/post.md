Designers are quietly shifting from pixel-pushers to AI-curators.

New AI co-pilot features inside mainstream design tools now handle first-draft wireframes, layout variations, and copy placeholders — work that used to eat the first two days of a project. The designer's job is tilting toward judgment: curating options, stress-testing flows, and deciding what ships.

That shift changes the skill premium. Speed with Figma shortcuts matters less; taste, critique, and the ability to interrogate an AI-generated draft matter more. Teams I've spoken with are already rewriting job descriptions around it — "proficiency in AI-assisted workflows" is showing up in requirements, not just nice-to-haves.

The open question isn't whether to use the tools; it's whether designers keep the decision-making seat or quietly hand it over.
